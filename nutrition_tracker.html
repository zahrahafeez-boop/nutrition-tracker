PMOS notes
Personalised guidance based on your profile
Protein target — why 110g?
Higher protein intake helps stabilise blood sugar, reduce cravings driven by oestrogen fluctuation, and preserve muscle during a deficit. Aim to spread it across 3 meals rather than loading it all at dinner.
Berberine — timing
400mg with each of your 3 main meals is more effective and gentler on your gut than 1200mg in one go. It mimics metformin for insulin sensitivity — key for PMOS hormone regulation. Avoid combining with other blood sugar lowering supplements.
Magnesium — why it matters
Most women with PMOS are low in magnesium. Target 310mg/day from food (dark leafy greens, pumpkin seeds, dark chocolate) and/or supplement. It helps with sleep, cortisol, and insulin sensitivity.
Meal timing
Inconsistent meal timing worsens insulin resistance in PMOS. Try to eat within a 1hr window each day — e.g. always break fast between 12–1pm, lunch 3–4pm, dinner 7–8pm. The exact times matter less than the consistency.
Foods that help
Cinnamon (you're already doing this ✓), apple cider vinegar before meals, flaxseeds for oestrogen balance, dark leafy greens for iron + magnesium, fatty fish for omega-3 anti-inflammation.
Foods to limit
Refined carbs (maida, white bread, packaged biscuits), excess dairy if you notice bloating, high-glycaemic fruits like mango on their own. Pair fruit with protein or fat to slow glucose response.
